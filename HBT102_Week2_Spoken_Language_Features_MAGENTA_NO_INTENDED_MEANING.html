Explanation: Spoken vs Written Language

A closer look at how the two forms of language differ.

  • Grammar: spoken language uses flexible grammar, often with incomplete sentences. Written language follows strict, planned grammar.
  • Feedback: spoken language gets immediate feedback from a listener. Written language has delayed feedback from a reader.
  • Medium: spoken language is voice-based, relying on sound. Written language is text-based, relying on visual symbols on a page or screen.

💡 Key Points

Spoken language is spontaneous, contextual, and allows immediate feedback; written language is planned and revisable. Because interpreters work with live speech, they must capture not just the words but the tone, pauses, and intent behind them.

Key Points

💡 Key Points

  • Spoken language is spontaneous, contextual, interactive, and dynamic — very different from planned, revisable written language.
  • Prosody (intonation, stress, rhythm, and tone) carries meaning that words alone don't capture, and interpreters must render it faithfully.
  • Word and sentence stress can completely change meaning or focus — small shifts like PERmit vs perMIT matter.
